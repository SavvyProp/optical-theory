# Stimulated Raman scattering

Archived for a possible future paper on optical floating-point encoding. With an undepleted pump, the Stokes probe power scales as `P_out/P_off ≈ exp(κ P_pump)`. Could a pump that encodes an exponent apply a calibrated factor of `2^e` to a probe carrying the significand magnitude in its power? The usable exponent range, scaling precision and addition of values with different exponents are open questions. [Floating-point encoding precedents](../notes/scattering_multipliers.md#floating-point-encodings)

## Raman gain and multiplication

In the undepleted-pump regime the Stokes output power is approximately the input probe power multiplied by an exponential in pump power. A fixed or optically programmed weight can therefore multiply a positive input intensity after calibration. With two freely varying, unencoded powers, the **change** in probe power approximates their product at weak gain. The transmitted probe provides a baseline. Experiments have verified the bilinear intensity change, and a separate silicon waveguide achieved **over +3 dB net CW gain at about 700 mW in-guide pump power**. [Two-input intensity measurement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3576036/) · [Net silicon Raman gain](https://doi.org/10.1364/OPEX.13.000519)

**At the target pump power.** Huang et al. measured +1.1 dB pump-on/off Raman gain with 48 mW CW coupled into a 1.9 cm silicon-core fiber. Using their 8.9 dB passive insertion loss at 1550 nm as an estimate at the 1546.3 nm gain peak, a 100 µW probe gives about 12.9 µW out with the pump off and 16.6 µW with it on. The 3.7 µW change is −14.3 dB relative to the input probe. At the internal fiber reference planes their estimated 1 dB/cm propagation loss instead gives 1.9 dB passive loss, −0.8 dB total transmission with pumping and −7.3 dB for the pump-induced power change. This change rides on the transmitted probe. If the desired encoding uses the full scaled probe, a calibrated scalar weight can use the full output, and a zero weight needs a way to extinguish it. [Huang et al., Fig. 5](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0060108)

Huang et al. later measured **+9 dB pump-on/off** at 1546.7 nm with a 96 mW coupled CW pump and a 100 µW signal in a silicon-core fiber with a 7 cm waist. Their 7.5 cm total length and estimated 0.2 dB/cm propagation loss imply about **+7.5 dB between the guide ends**. With the reported 9.2 dB insertion loss, the external signal gain was about **−0.2 dB**. The gain measurement used a single CW probe. [Huang et al. (2025), Table I and Fig. 5](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0245083)

**Speed of silicon Raman gain.** The intrinsic 105 GHz Raman linewidth gives a vibrational coherence time of about 3.0 ps. In a 7 mm silicon waveguide, Xu et al. measured a 6.3 ps-wide gain window using 3.5 ps pump and 3.7 ps probe pulses. They measured less than 2 ps of relative delay from the different propagation speeds, and observed additional absorption from free carriers whose lifetime was about 1 ns. The pump was 20.7 W peak and 5.9 mW average at the input taper. Repeating that Gaussian pump pulse every 10 ps would require about 7.7 W average. The low-power CW gain and picosecond gain window come from separate experiments. For 10 ps slots, evaluate gain and history-dependent error on consecutive symbols at the intended peak and average powers. [Xu et al., Fig. 1](https://doi.org/10.1364/OPEX.12.004437) · [Huang et al., Fig. 5](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0060108)

The ideal fractional change is `exp(u) − 1`, with `u` proportional to pump power. At a 10% change relative to the unpumped probe, the raw response is 4.9% above the linear approximation `u`. Another option is to encode inputs on several undepleted pumps. Their contributions then add in the logarithm of each probe's gain, giving a weighted sum followed by an exponential. The weights depend on the pump–probe frequency separations and mode overlap. Independent rows would need additional controls. [Coupled Raman power equations](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44172-024-00253-w) · [Programmable multiband Raman gain](https://doi.org/10.1109/JLT.2020.3033768)

One measured Raman spectral projection used two alternating pump masks to weight different parts of a sample's vibrational spectrum. A photodiode and lock-in amplifier recovered their signed difference on a single Stokes beam. This demonstrates frequency-weighted summation with electrical readout. [Freudiger et al., Fig. 3](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphoton.2010.294)

## Structured matrices with Raman gain

The original design goal was a linear field MVM with many inputs and outputs in one nonlinear waveguide. A periodically pumped comb was the candidate for a Toeplitz or Hankel matrix with shared weights. The static two-port interferometer provides a gain benchmark. Even with gain on every wavelength, its couplers mix only the two spatial inputs at each wavelength, giving one independent 2 × 2 block per tooth.

**Comb convolution by periodic gain.** Encode the vector in coherent Stokes field amplitudes on a grid of spacing Δf. Shape the optical pump so the *field* transmission is periodic, `a(t) = Σ_k a[k] exp(2πikΔf t)`. When the Raman response is uniform over the occupied spectrum and follows the pump, multiplication of the field by a(t) gives `y[j] = Σ_i a[j−i] x[i]`. Reversing input index order gives a Hankel matrix with entries `a[j+i−(N−1)]`. With pump-induced phase compensated, a real positive field gain gives conjugate-symmetric taps and a positive-definite Toeplitz factor. Its large diagonal term can serve as a learned skip connection. A calibrated sinusoidal field gain gives exactly three taps in this model. The through carrier is the diagonal weight, and only the two outer shifts of a finite comb can leave the selected band. Fan et al. demonstrated comb convolution with an electro-optically modulated resonator. Raman gain as the modulator is a proposed implementation. [Fan et al., Fig. 1](https://web.stanford.edu/~llfan/projects/sci_adv_2023.pdf) · [Pulsed silicon Raman propagation equations](https://labsites.rochester.edu/agrawal/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/paper_2011_07.pdf)

**Choosing nonuniform data teeth.** With inputs and outputs on teeth `s[i]`, the same field convolution gives the selected matrix `W[j,i] = a[s[j]−s[i]]`. On teeth {0, 1, 3}, three real cosine harmonics can weight the three unordered pairs separately around a common diagonal, within the gain range. Count `m[k]` ordered data pairs with offset k. Averaging over one equally likely lit input, the fraction of generated sideband power retained is `Σ_{k≠0} m[k]|a[k]|² / (N Σ_{k≠0}|a[k]|²)`. If all positive pair spacings are unique and only those offsets and their negatives are driven, this fraction is 1/N. For nearest-neighbor mixing on N consecutive teeth it is (N−1)/N. These fractions exclude the carrier and are independent of absolute Raman gain. This is a consequence of the ideal common-gain model. [Frequency-convolution precedent](https://web.stanford.edu/~llfan/projects/sci_adv_2023.pdf)

**Two-port field mixer.** Two balanced couplers form a sum and a difference of same-carrier spatial inputs. Pump the sum arm, match the passive arm transmissions and phases, then recombine and retain both ports. Write `l = √τ` and `h = √(τR)` for the unpumped and pumped field gains, with R the pump-on/off power ratio and τ passive power transmission. The resulting 2 × 2 matrix has diagonal entries `(h+l)/2` and cross entries `(h−l)/2`. Its sum and difference eigenvectors have gains h and l. With N spatial modes a Fourier transform, independently pumped gain mask and inverse transform implement a circulant field matrix. Phase control and extinction extend the mask to complex or zero eigenvalues. Input reversal yields cyclic Hankel. An ordinary finite Toeplitz/Hankel embedding can use 2N−1 modes with zero padding and output selection. A frequency-comb implementation requires coherent frequency mixing to perform those Fourier transforms. [Spatial Fourier convolution experiment](https://pure.tue.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/316731936/Optical_4F_correlator_for_Acceleration_of_Convolutional_Neural_Networks.pdf)

**Power per coefficient.** Assume Huang et al.’s 9 dB CW on/off gain at 96 mW is available in a single pumped arm or at the crest of the gain waveform. For the comb set `a(t) = (h+l)/2 + [(h−l)/2] cos(2πΔf t)`, so each adjacent field coefficient is `(h−l)/4`. Values below are signal power gains from one lit input to one output, excluding coupler excess loss and pump-induced phase error. The first column uses 1.5 dB propagation loss, the second the reported 9.2 dB external insertion loss. [Huang et al., Table I and Fig. 5](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0245083)

| Useful path | Between guide ends | With reference-device insertion loss |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Through path, both designs | +4.1 dB | −3.6 dB |
| Each neighbor of an interior comb tooth | −8.3 dB | −16.0 dB |
| Cross port of the 2 × 2 mixer | −2.3 dB | −10.0 dB |

For real amplitude modulation confined to [l,h], Parseval gives total off-diagonal power per lit tooth `Σₖ≠₀ |aₖ|² = Var(a) ≤ (h−l)²/4`, normalized to input power. Sharing this budget equally among 63 cross outputs gives at most −20.3 dB each between guide ends for the stated gain range. The projected gains assume quiet undepleted pumping. At 100 GHz spacing the 64 input teeth span 6.3 THz, while the intrinsic crystalline silicon Raman line is about 105 GHz wide. Four teeth at 10 GHz spacing span 30 GHz and have a 100 ps Fourier period. A dynamic test should include the complex Raman susceptibility, pump–signal walk-off, pump-induced phase, carrier absorption and spontaneous noise. [Silicon Raman bandwidth and time response](https://doi.org/10.1364/OPEX.12.004437)

**Direct mixing of powers.** With data on undepleted higher-frequency pump teeth and fixed Stokes seeds, `ln(Sⱼ,out/Sⱼ,off) = Σᵢ Kⱼᵢ Pᵢ`. For uniform grids and equal mode overlaps, K is a sampled Raman gain line depending on i−j. The linear result is in log power. At 100 GHz spacing a 105 GHz Lorentzian gives normalized coupling coefficients 1, 0.216 and 0.064 at offsets 0, 1 and 2. This kernel is set by the medium. [Silicon Raman linewidth](https://doi.org/10.1364/OL.30.000035) · [Multi-pump Raman equations](https://photonics.umbc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/688/2022/02/PAJ183.pdf)

Spatial pump and Stokes modes in one multimode guide give a different kernel K set by pairwise intensity overlap. Christensen et al. measured normalized same-group overlaps of 1 and 0.56 and a cross-group overlap of 0.47. The unequal diagonal entries break Toeplitz symmetry. Zitelli et al. amplified four signal groups simultaneously using five pump modes, measuring +2.6 dB mean co-propagating pump-on/off gain with 1 W total coupled pump power in 8 km of fiber. For computing with data on the pump modes this suggests a fixed positive matrix in log gain, or an exponential activation in raw Stokes power. The communication experiments measured modal gains. Independent weight programming and 10 ps arithmetic precision remain open. [Christensen et al., Eqs. 3–5 and Table 1](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep34693) · [Zitelli et al., Table 1](https://iris.uniroma1.it/retrieve/2d283f5b-b6f6-4d19-bfc0-b8fcbb4d5637/Zitelli_Inter-modal_2024.pdf)

Alternatively, use weak data pumps and strong Stokes seeds whose fractional changes stay small. Let bⱼᵢ be the pump-power depletion rate per unit length into seed j, proportional to its power and the Raman line at that detuning, and bᵢ = Σⱼ bⱼᵢ. In a lossless guide with constant seeds, integrating the pump decay gives a linear map of *incremental* Stokes powers with entries `(νₛⱼ/νₚᵢ) (bⱼᵢ/bᵢ) [1−exp(−bᵢL)]` for bᵢ>0. Each pump photon consumed creates one Stokes photon. At 1431→1547 nm, 90% pump depletion shared among four equal outputs would give −6.8 dB each. The same transfer split 64 ways gives −18.9 dB each. These are conditional energy budgets. For a 200 µW data input the four-way example gives 41.6 µW above each seed. A coherent 24 mW seed at 1550 nm has about 17.5 µW RMS shot noise in an ideal 10 ps direct power read, already 42% of that increment with an exactly known baseline. A subsequent optical layer needs a way to use the increment. [Photon balance and stimulated Raman gain/loss](https://sunneyxielab.org/data/publications/2011/2011_Freudiger_NPhoton_2010.294.pdf) · [Coupled power equations](https://photonics.umbc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/688/2022/02/PAJ183.pdf)

## Noise for a 10 ps operation

For a 200 µW probe in one matched mode and polarization at 1550 nm, ideal direct Raman gain of +5.7 dB gives 743 µW output and a modeled 1.09% relative RMS pulse-energy fluctuation. That case uses the full amplified probe as the result. The estimates below also cover reading the pump-induced change in probe power. The FWM comparison uses the same read duration and final signal power. [Amplified FWM comparison](../notes/scattering_multipliers.md#amplified-fwm-in-one-10-ps-operation)

The ideal lossless Raman model gives `m_R = (G_R − 1)(1 + n_T)` spontaneous photons per output mode. At a 15.6 THz shift and 300 K the thermal phonon occupation is 0.090, giving 2.96 noise photons at +5.7 dB. If the product is the intensity change `P_on − P_off`, its relative RMS increases by `P_on/(P_on − P_off)` for an exactly known baseline. This factor is 4.47 at +1.1 dB on/off and 1.14 at +9 dB. An independently measured baseline adds variance. Strong Raman gain requires calibration of the exponential weight response. The scalar estimate assumes flat gain over the signal mode. A matrix comparison also needs the data and pump power available to each independently weighted path. [Rottwitt et al., Eqs. 41–46](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/files/4214365/Rottwitt.pdf) · [Silicon Raman amplifier noise model](https://opg.optica.org/jlt/abstract.cfm?uri=jlt-26-7-847)

For the 48 mW Raman experiment above, scale the seed to 200 µW at the guide input. The guide output is 166 µW on and 129 µW off. A +13.0 dB, 5 dB noise-figure amplifier scales the 37 µW difference to 743 µW. Model the measured on/off gain `R` and passive transmission `L` as uniform along the guide, with `T = LR`. This gives `m_R = (1 + n_T) ln(R) (T − 1)/ln(T) = 0.252` noise photons per mode before the erbium amplifier. The difference has **7.5%** relative RMS for an exactly known baseline and **9.7%** for two independent 10 ps on/off reads. These are model estimates from CW data. An optical cascade would need a way to extract or encode this difference. [Huang et al. (2021), Fig. 5](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0060108) · [Distributed amplifier noise, Eqs. 6–7](https://opg.optica.org/jlt/abstract.cfm?uri=jlt-26-7-847)
